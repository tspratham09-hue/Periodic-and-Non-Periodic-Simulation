📖 Signals & Systems Viva Explanation Guide (Periodic vs. Non-Periodic)

1. Why Do Periodic and Non-Periodic Signals Behave Differently?

  • Periodic Signal x̃(t) = x̃(t + T0): Repeats continuously over (-∞, ∞). Because it repeats every T0 seconds, only sinusoids at exact integer multiples of the fundamental frequency (ω0 = 2π / T0) constructively interfere. All other frequencies cancel out, producing a Discrete Line Spectrum (CTFS).
  • Non-Periodic Signal x(t): Exists only for a finite duration τ and never repeats (equivalent to T0 → ∞). Synthesizing a signal that stays exactly zero outside [-τ/2, τ/2] requires an uncountably infinite continuum of frequencies, producing a Continuous Spectrum X(jω) (CTFT).

2. Mathematical Bridge Equation (CTFS ↔ CTFT)

CTFT of Single Pulse: X(jω) = ∫ x(t) e^(-jωt) dt = A·τ·sinc(ωτ / 2π)
CTFS of Periodic Train: ck = (1 / T0) ∫ x̃(t) e^(-jkω0t) dt = (1 / T0) · X(j·kω0)
Bridge Relation: T0 · ck = X(jω) |_(ω = kω0)

3. How Signal Modifications Affect the Spectrum

  • Increasing Period T0: Fundamental spacing ω0 = 2π/T0 shrinks. The discrete harmonic stems pack closer together and merge into the continuous CTFT envelope as T0 → ∞.
  • Decreasing Pulse Width τ (Time Compression): By the Time-Frequency Scaling Property, compressing a pulse in time expands its spectrum in frequency (Main Lobe Width = 4π/τ).
  • Time Shifting x(t - t0): Magnitude |X(jω)| and |ck| remain completely unchanged; phase shifts linearly by -ω·t0 (twisting the complex spectrum into a 3D helix).
  • Edge Smoothness (Rectangular vs. Triangular): Sharp discontinuities in a rectangular pulse cause slow 1/ω side-lobe decay and ~9% Gibbs overshoot. A smooth triangular pulse decays faster at 1/ω² (sinc²).
